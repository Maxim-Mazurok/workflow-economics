THE CHARTS HAVE TO EARN THEIR SHAPE

A model you can inspect.

A deterministic fluid approximation to a recurring stream of tasks. Research motivates the mechanisms; it does not calibrate these coefficients.

MODEL 1.1
Work arrives→Produce + review→Useful / defective / retry→Capability + debt↻

A task is one demand opportunity with a defined requirements test. Candidates may fail and be retried; useful output earns value once. Shipped defects consume an opportunity and incur a separate loss.

A workflow is a vector of resource choices: setup, task ownership, production, review, repair, compute, tuning, upkeep, and concurrency. Names never appear in the equations, except to identify the manual comparator.

Quality is a latent probability of satisfying that test, not a claim that creativity has a universal score. Verification detects some failures, and repaired work can still fail. Use observed audits to estimate these inputs.

01 / Resources, learning, and debt

K ← [1 − (1 − K) exp(−Δlearning)] exp(−drift · Δt)Reusable capability K starts at 0 for AI workflows. Learning = (setup hours + tuningEffect × tuning hours + feedbackEffect × review hours) ÷ [learningScale × (1 + 2 × variability)]. Tuning per week = initial tuning × exp(−attempts / tuningDecay).

D ← max(0, D − ½ × upkeep hours) + new debtD is remediation hours. New debt = coupling × manual task hours × [escapedDebt × escaped defects + structuralDebt × released results × (1 − craftsmanship)]. Craftsmanship = K for autonomous drafts and 0.9 for expert-led work. Mixed workflows sum debt from each branch’s released outputs separately. Half of upkeep maintains infrastructure; half retires debt. Coupling = 0 means no accumulated debt.

b = D / (debtScale × manual task hours)A dimensionless burden. It raises production, review, repair, and machine effort, and reduces first-pass correctness. Debt is not charged as cash a second time; only actual effort and external escaped-defect loss are charged.

02 / Autonomous output and expert-led assistance

pAI = sigmoid(logit(aiFit) + computeGain · ln(compute) + capabilityGain · K − noveltyPenalty · variability · (1 − K) − debtQuality · ln(1 + b))
pExpert = sigmoid(logit(manualFit) − noveltyPenalty · variability · 0.35 − debtQuality · ln(1 + b))Autonomous AI produces a complete candidate; its correctness depends on autonomous first-pass fit. In expert-led work, AI helps with subtasks while the expert remains responsible for the result. That branch uses the expert’s correctness, not standalone AI correctness. The model assumes the expert maintains judgment and review discipline; it does not claim that automation bias or bad advice cannot harm real experts.

d = 1 − exp(−detectionGain × review × verifiability × (1 + 0.4 × craftsmanship))
f = repairSuccess / (1 + 0.45 × variability + 0.2 × b)
g = p + (1 − p) × d × repairShare × f
e = (1 − p) × (1 − d)Calculated independently for each route: craftsmanship is K for autonomous drafts and 0.9 for expert-led work. Detection d catches some defects; a fraction is repaired with success f. g is useful yield and e escaped-defect yield. Rejected candidates return to the backlog. Correct work is assumed not to be falsely rejected.

gMixed = autonomy × gAI + (1 − autonomy) × gExpert
eMixed = autonomy × eAI + (1 − autonomy) × eExpertAutonomy is the fraction of candidate tasks routed to autonomous drafting. 0% is fully expert-led assistance; 100% is fully autonomous drafting followed by review. The same weighted sum applies to labor and branch-specific structural debt. Manual has no assistance and no autonomous branch. Defaults are role assumptions, not measured shares.

03 / Human time, interactive acceleration, and capacity

saving = assistSaving × (0.8 + 0.2 × K) / (1 + 0.2 × variability)
interaction = assistOverhead × [1 + variability × (1 − K)]
hExpertProduction = h₀ × (1 − saving + interaction) × (1 + debtFriction × b)Interactive assistance reduces expert production time through research, navigation, documentation, and implementation help. Directing and checking assistance takes additional time. The saving and overhead parameters are independent of autonomous result correctness. If interaction costs exceed savings, assistance is slower. For manual work, saving and interaction are zero and the production multiplier is its direct-effort setting.

hAIProduction = h₀ × [direct + 0.12 × variability × (1 − K)] × (1 + debtFriction × b)
hreview = h₀ × review × (1 + 0.3 × b)
hrepair = h₀ × 0.65 × caughtDefects × repairShare × (1 + 0.4 × variability + 0.5 × b)h₀ is standard manual production time. Autonomous task share mixes expert and autonomous production hours. Review and repair are still charged. The thoughtful assistance preset keeps the manual expert’s review and repair discipline; its setup, tuning, API cost, and interaction time are additional costs.

humanCapacity = teamSize × hoursPerPerson
m = machineTime × compute × (1 + 0.3 × b) / (1 + 0.5 × K)
API cost / candidate = aiPrice × compute × (1 + 0.3 × b)
effective concurrency = 1 / [serial + (1 − serial) / parallel]Team size exposes the human resource pool. Hours per person means time allocated to this workload, not paid hours. This remains a pooled-capacity model with equally capable people; individual scheduling and coordination overhead are not modeled. Machine concurrency does not remove human gates or shorten a single sequential call.

attempts = min(available human hours / h, available machine hours × effective concurrency / m, backlog / (g + e))Setup uses the human pool until complete. Tuning and upkeep share the remaining budget proportionally if they cannot both fit. Production uses what remains. Machine capacity is prorated to the interval after setup. Useful work = attempts × g; releases = attempts × (g + e). Manual work has no machine cost or constraint.

04 / Metrics, value, and a fair baseline

quality = Σ useful / Σ released
human effort / useful = Σ all human hours / Σ useful
cost / useful = (Σ labor opportunity cost + Σ API spend + Σ external defect loss) / Σ usefulRatios are undefined until useful output exists; they are never plotted as zero. Cumulative effort includes the entire setup investment. “Current-period” effort includes overhead in that interval. “Next-result labor” excludes sunk setup and recurring overhead, and is labeled accordingly.

surplus per interval = value × useful − failureLoss × escaped − humanPrice × active hours − API spend
NPV = Σ surplus / (1 + annual discount rate)^(week / 52)
value relative to manual = NPVworkflow − NPVmanualThe comparator faces identical arrivals, calendar horizon, human capacity, and economic assumptions. Unequal delivered quantity is valued explicitly. This is incremental economic surplus, not a percentage ROI. A zero crossing means parity with manual work at that date, not a contest between two AI strategies. No hard-coded break-even marker is drawn.

average throughput = cumulative useful / elapsed weeks
current throughput = useful in the latest interval / interval lengthThe 3D plot uses averages since the start, including setup downtime. Time plots use the metric in their selector. Backlog is shown separately from processing speed; no queueing steady state is assumed.

05 / Numerical method, uncertainty, and limits

Playback interpolates accumulated quantities between simulation records, then recomputes per-result ratios. Its fixed axes, optional log(1 + dollars) cost scale, and fade-in after one expected useful result are display choices only; accounting starts immediately, and static views retain all defined values. The main simulation uses 0.03125-week increments; surfaces and Monte Carlo use 0.0625-week increments. It tracks expected flows, so fractional tasks and smooth curves are deliberate. It is suitable for reasoning about recurring workloads, not the finish time of one unique task. Events inside an interval and exact task latency are not resolved. A small convergence test is included with the source.

The uncertainty run starts automatically with 160 paired draws. A fresh random seed is chosen on page load; New draw replaces it. Reusing a displayed seed exactly reproduces results for the same assumptions. Returning to the tab reuses the cached run. Saved assumption files include seed and spread. Positive coefficients receive mean-preserving lognormal multipliers exp(σZ − σ²/2); fit probabilities and the interactive saving fraction receive normal perturbations on their log-odds. Z is a standard normal draw, independent across varied parameters. Autonomous and manual task fit, interactive savings, interaction overhead, learning scale, defect-induced debt, debt friction, API price, useful-output value, and failure loss vary. This is parameter sensitivity, not simulated random task failures, and the assumed independence may be wrong.

Limits: one average task class; no correlated failures, permanent unsolvable task class, exact task queue, false-positive review, delayed defect discovery, deadlines, output expiry, catastrophic loss tails, labor skill heterogeneity, provider rate limits, or infrastructure cash costs. Useful results have constant marginal value up to the demand cap. Quality cannot be inferred just from acceptance. These omissions matter especially for creative selection pipelines and safety-critical work.

To calibrate: measure first-pass success, audited escapes, reviewer and repair hours, setup/tuning/upkeep hours, machine latency and spend, work arrival rate, and useful demand. Fit the mechanisms to held-out history and compare predictions. Until then, the display is a scenario laboratory, not a forecast.

06 / Change the structural coefficients

These shared assumptions control the shape of the model. Changing them reruns every workflow. Fixed constants are documented in the equations above and editable in the model source.

EVIDENCE, WITH ITS BOUNDARIES

Why these mechanisms belong in the model

01 / CONTEXT

Productivity effects are heterogeneous.

A customer-support study found a 15% average productivity gain with substantial differences across workers. That supports context-sensitive inputs, not a blanket speed multiplier.

Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond · Generative AI at Work ↗
02 / TASK FIT

Fast and correct are separate outcomes.

The HBS/BCG experiment found gains within the tested AI frontier and reduced correctness on an outside-frontier task. More difficult or novel tasks need separate quality and verification assumptions.

Dell’Acqua et al. · Jagged Technological Frontier ↗
03 / MEASUREMENT

Perceived speed is not measured speed.

METR’s early-2025 study found a slowdown in a specific setting. Its February 2026 update reports that selection and measurement problems make newer effect sizes unreliable. Neither result fixes a universal AI curve.

METR · Developer productivity experiment update ↗
04 / SYSTEM COST

Automation leaves an ongoing system to maintain.

Research on ML systems describes dependencies, feedback loops, and maintenance burdens. It motivates a debt state; it does not establish the numerical debt rates used here or prove that vibe coding must deteriorate.

Sculley et al. · Hidden Technical Debt ↗
05 / INVESTMENT

Useful infrastructure takes real work.

The productivity J-curve literature examines complementary intangible investment and measurement. It motivates explicit setup accounting, not a guaranteed private ROI curve or eventual payback.

Brynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson · Productivity J-Curve ↗
